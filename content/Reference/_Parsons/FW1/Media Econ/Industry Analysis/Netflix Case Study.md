- Founded on an early bet by Hastings and Randolph on the commercial viability of the DVD versus the VHS
	- Sharper quality, more compact, less likely to break, etc.
- Launched first online rental service in 1998
- Overhead on sending DVDs with no returning customers put them in a hole
- Moved to a subscription model the next year: one flat fee for unlimited rentals
##### Blockbuster and initial IPO (2000-2006)
- Netflix offered to sell to Blockbuster as a digital wing of the company
	- Blockbuster functions on a physical location/franchise model that wouldn't allow other models that skipped the in person experience to work
	- Local franchises didn't want to lose their profitability from in person purchases
- Netflix couldn't purchase popular titles in mass due to licensing fees
	- Created *Cinematch* to guide users to similar, lesser known titles that fit their preferences
	- Introduced data that could reflect user taste and *influence their behavior of viewership*
- Profitability in 2003 and over 6 million subscribers. by 2006 allowed them to produce over night delivery
#### Streaming and Quickster (2007-2011)
- Hasting introduced a limited run of 1,000 movies to stream
- Popularity led to a split: *Quickster* for DVD delivery and *Netflix* for streaming
- Split raised cost by 60% and lost 800,000 subscribers in Q1
#### Original Content/Data Driven Decisions (2012-2015)
- Netflix functioned on a "re-run" model where original content post theatrical release could be bought a added to streaming for viewers
- As Netflix grew in subscribers, Hollywood began to up their licensing fees
- Netflix moved more quickly towards original content backed by 10+ years of viewer data with *House of Cards, Orange is the New Black and Stranger Things*
	- Green lit multiple seasons based on forecast rather than running pilots before purchasing
	- Binge model of releasing entire season improved engagement across word of mouth and socials
- Forecast model gave directors a new [[Value Chain]]
	- more creative freedom (less constraints with budget or required success across seas)
	- less upfront transparency on earnings (data was kept internal and purchase was upfront)
#### Global Expansion/Pandemic Boom (2016-2020)
- Netflix simultaneously expanded to over 130 countries (totaling 190)
- Created [[Local-for-global model]] that created foreign properties (*Squid Game*, *Lupin*) that were shared with worldwide audiences
	- Recommendation algorithm spiked titles like *Squid Game* (~$900 million worldwide)
- Pandemic added over 36 million subscribers and increased content budget to over $18 billion
#### Ad Tier, Live Events, Password Crackdown (2022-2025)
---
- Audience attention split between streaming (roughly 50%), over air broadcast (20%), cable (~23%) and physical copy viewers (~5%)
- Focus became profitability, customer [[Churn]] and improvements on average user profits
- Live sports remained locked with legacy TV with multi-million dollar deals
- Streamers don't have the bandwidth to stream live sports
- **~5.1 platforms**: average household streaming platforms
- Ad supported tiers allowed many to utilize streaming at a more affordable rate (most popular amongst households)
#### Netflix Competitors
- **Legacy Studios**
	- Studios like Sony became 'arms dealers' of IP to distributors like Netflix
	- PRO:
		- Able to sidestep enormous cost for user acquisition/distribution
		- Can sidestep strikes with flexibility
	- CON:
		- Capped profitability on IP
		- eg. *K Pop Demonhunters* had an initial fee for purchase, but is now a Netflix IP for sequels, merch and any in person experiences
- **Integrated Content Producers**
	- Companies like Disney and Amazon that have an integrated system in place that feeds their streaming model
		- Disney has all of its IP, Hulu, ESPN, parks and cruises to name a few that help to create an ecosystem for subscribers to move within
		- Amazon uses Prime as a plus to their eCommerce platform
		- Less pressure for profitability and deeper pockets helps them bid on IP like Sports as an addition to their previous model
- **Entertainment Platforms**
	- Youtube and TikTok
		- User Generated platforms that reach profitability through ads or in app purchases
