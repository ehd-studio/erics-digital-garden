First described by Michael Porter in his 1979 _Harvard Business Review_ article, the **Five Forces framework** provides a model for understanding the competitive forces at work within an industry and how economic value is divided among industry actors. A Five Forces analysis helps companies evaluate industry attractiveness, anticipate competitive shifts, choose which markets to enter, and position themselves for sustained profitability.

---

### The Five Competitive Forces

1. **Threat of New Entrants**
    
    - **Impact:** New entrants bring additional production capacity and pressure prices and costs, placing a natural cap on an industry’s profit potential. Existing players are often forced to keep prices down or spend more to retain customers.
    - **Key Drivers:** The level of threat depends on **barriers to entry**—including economies of scale, the cost of building brand awareness, access to distribution channels, and regulatory restrictions—as well as the scale and resources of potential entrants.
2. **Bargaining Power of Suppliers**
    
    - **Impact:** Powerful suppliers use negotiating leverage to charge higher prices or demand more favorable terms, shifting profit away from industry participants.
    - **Key Drivers:** Supplier power is highest when inputs are concentrated among one or two suppliers, or when switching suppliers is costly or time-consuming.
3. **Bargaining Power of Buyers**
    
    - **Impact:** Powerful customers use their clout to force prices down or demand higher quality and additional services at existing price points.
    - **Key Drivers:** Buyer leverage is strongest when buyers are large relative to sellers, products are standardized or undifferentiated, items represent a significant fraction of the buyer's cost structure, and buyer switching costs are low.
4. **Threat of Substitute Products or Services**
    
    - **Impact:** A substitute satisfies the same underlying customer need through a different approach (e.g., videoconferencing substituting for business travel, or email for express mail).
    - **Key Drivers:** The threat is high if the substitute delivers an attractive price-to-performance trade-off and switching costs for buyers are low.
5. **Rivalry Among Existing Competitors**
    
    - **Impact:** Intense rivalry drives down prices or raises the cost of competing (such as elevated ad spend or customer support expenses), eroding overall industry profitability.
    - **Key Drivers:** Rivalry is fierce when competitors are numerous or equal in size, industry growth is slow, fixed costs create incentives for price cutting, exit barriers are high, or rivals are committed to contrasting strategic goals.

---

### Key Industry Dynamics & Strategy

The article emphasizes that **industry structure is dynamic rather than static**. Over time, buyer or supplier power fluctuates, technological or managerial innovations alter entry and substitution threats, and regulatory shifts reconfigure competitive rules. Evaluating the Five Forces allows firms to anticipate structural changes, influence how industry rules evolve, and secure a resilient strategic position.
