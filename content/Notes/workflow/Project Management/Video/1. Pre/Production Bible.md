A **production bible** (also called a series bible, TV show bible, or format bible) is a comprehensive reference document that serves as the master blueprint and "single source of truth" for a film, television series, animated project, or multimedia property. It combines creative vision with logistical planning to ensure that writers, directors, producers, designers, and crew members operate with a shared understanding.

In practice, a production bible serves a dual purpose:

- **As a Pitch & Business Tool:** It demonstrates a project's long-term narrative engine, cross-platform viability, and structural depth to studio executives, networks, or investors.
- **As an Operational Living Document:** Created in development, it is continually revised through pre-production, filming, and post-production to maintain continuity and track assets.

---

### Key Materials & Components Required for a Production Bible

While exact contents vary depending on whether the document leans toward a sales pitch or physical production management, a complete production bible typically incorporates materials across five main categories:

#### 1. Core Concept & Narrative Framework

- **Title & Metadata:** Project title, format specifications (e.g., episode count and length), genre, target audience, creator details, and copyright notices.
- **Logline & Premise:** A 1–2 sentence hook and an expanded 1-page concept summary defining the "story engine" driving the series.
- **Story Outlines:** A prose summary of the pilot episode, sample episode synopses (typically 3–10 episodes), and maps of the Season 1 arc and multi-season direction.

#### 2. World-Building & Creative Guidelines

- **World Rules & Lore:** Descriptions of primary settings, social dynamics, history, and the explicit physical or metaphysical rules governing the universe (e.g., magic limitations, technology rules).
- **Visual Style & Tone Guides:** Concept artwork, mood boards, color palettes, visual references, lighting direction, and music/audio notes.
- **Storyboards & Pre-Vis:** Panel illustrations, shot compositions, and technical pre-visualization graphics.

#### 3. Character & Talent Materials

- **Character Profiles:** Bios for series regulars detailing external goals, internal emotional needs, character contradictions, backstories, and arc trajectories.
- **Cast & Crew Directories:** Contact sheets, assigned roles, availability status, and paperwork/contract tracking for talent and crew.

#### 4. Production Logistics & Operational Data

- **Script Breakdown:** Element-by-element breakdown of scripts categorizing scenes, required props, wardrobe, makeup, visual effects, and special gear.
- **Schedules & Budgets:** Master production timelines, daily shooting schedules, call sheet templates, and budget allocations.
- **Location & Supplier Directories:** Storage and filming location specs, backup location plans, equipment vendor lists, pickup/return schedules, and Certificates of Insurance (COI).

#### 5. Legal, Technical & Business Specifications

- **Technical Standards:** Specifications for cameras, lenses, lighting setups, file-naming conventions, and safety protocols.
- **Legal & Delivery Checklists:** Trademark clearances, music rights, talent release forms, and technical delivery requirements (e.g., runtime, captions, video codecs).
- **Network Fit & Strategy:** Demographic market analysis, network complement context, and cross-platform or international format expansion potential.
