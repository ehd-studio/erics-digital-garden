In 2025, Baseball saw the fruits of its labor. The World Series between the LA Dodgers and the Toronto Blue Jays saw its highest viewership numbers since 1992 with recurrent viewership average on the higher end of 34 million [Forbes](https://www.forbes.com/sites/maurybrown/2025/11/06/everything-to-know-about-the-record-2025-world-series-viewership/). Game 7 alone had an average viewership of 51 million across US, Canada and Japan [MLB Press Release](https://www.mlb.com/press-release/press-release-51-million-average-viewers-watched-world-series-game-seven-in-u-s-canada-and-japan-combined). That was followed up the highest viewed and attended World Baseball Classic with a 23% increase in ticket sales and the final USA versus Venezuela drawing over 10 million people [MLB Press Release](https://www.mlb.com/news/world-baseball-classic-potential-format-improvements#:~:text=Capped%20by%20Venezuela's%20first%20WBC,compared%20to%20the%202017%20event.). The success is buttressed by improvement in game pace with rules like the pitch clock, global superstars like Shohei Ohtani and a Direct to Consumer model that makes games much more accessible. Post success and with an incoming Player's Union negotiation looming, many are asking how MLB and Baseball as a sport can try to sustain, or possible grow, its viewership to introduce a new generation to the game. 

This investigation intends to look at pass success, current explorations into expanding the game and possible markets moving forward for the future.
