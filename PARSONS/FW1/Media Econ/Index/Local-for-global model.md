In business and media economics, the **"local-for-global" model** refers to a strategic approach where localized innovations, assets, or content are created in specific regional environments and then exported or utilized to serve a global network.

The sources outline this model in two primary contexts: **technological R&D/innovation within multinational corporations (MNCs)** and **global media streaming strategies**. It is also closely linked to the broader concept of **transnational and "glocal" business strategies**.

---

1. Multinational Corporation (MNC) R&D and Innovation Strategy

In global business management, "local-for-global" describes an innovation framework where a multinational corporation distributes its R&D and technological expertise across a variety of host countries, rather than concentrating R&D in its home country (which is a _centre-for-global_ model) or isolating subsidiaries to only serve localized needs (a _local-for-local_ model).

This strategy allows a company to set up R&D operations in specific global regions that offer the most advanced, specialized, and culturally compatible environments for a particular technology. The localized knowledge generated in these subsidiaries then "spills backward" or is shared multidirectionally across the MNC's entire global network.

- **Silicon Valley (United States)** for semiconductor development.
- **Turin (Italy)** for automotive components.
- **India** for software engineering.
- **Philips**: The company established decentralized "centers of competence." For example, it moved its long-range technology development center from the United States to the Far East to capitalize on longer-term thinking and reward patterns.
- **IBM and Digital Equipment Corporation**: Relocated R&D facilities to Italy to leverage the highly successful, intuitive, and innovative local behaviors.

2. Media and Streaming Content Strategy (Glocalization)

In the streaming era, the "local-for-global" model has been popularized by **Netflix** as a central content-commissioning strategy.

Under this model, Netflix empowers local, independent producers in international offices to make decisions "in their own time zone, in their own country, and in their own language". The content is designed to first achieve massive impact and super-serve the home market. However, because the stories are deeply rooted in local culture and told with "cultural authenticity," they illuminate universal themes and can easily travel to find audiences all around the world via a global platform.

- **Squid Game (South Korea)**: A culturally specific Korean drama that became a massive global phenomenon.
- **Lupin (France)**: A highly specific French show filmed and set in France that resonated internationally and became one of Netflix's largest series.
- **La Casa de Papel / Money Heist (Spain)**: A Spanish television drama commissioned locally that captured global audiences.
- **Kingdom (South Korea)**: A localized historical zombie series that found success both in South Korea and traveled exceptionally well globally.

---

3. Transnational and "Glocal" Business Applications

The local-for-global mindset is also visible in **transnational strategies**, which seek "the best of both worlds" by balancing global integration (for scale and cost savings) with high local responsiveness (for customized consumer preferences). This is often called **glocalization**—doing business according to both local and global considerations.

- **KFC**: Leverages immense global purchasing power to lower chicken supply costs but implements highly customized local menus. For example, customers can buy **green curry chicken** at locations in Thailand or a **bento box meal** in Indonesia.
- **Costco**: Retains high-volume cost leadership globally while offering unique food court items tailored to regional tastes, such as **poutine** in Canada and **seafood pizza** in Taiwan.
- **Walmart**: Relies on strong international supply chains and purchasing power while carrying customized store layouts and inventories updated to match local preferences.
