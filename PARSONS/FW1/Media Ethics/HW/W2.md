### **Part 3: The Marcus Aurelius Test** 

_Reference: Ethics for the Real World, Chapter 4 (Negative vs. Positive Ethics)._

Puttnam cites the Roman Emperor’s motto: _"If it's not true, don't say it; if it's not right, don't do it"_.

3. **Negative vs. Positive Ethics:**
- **"If it's not true, don't say it"** is a **Negative Ethic** (a prohibition). It is a "bright line" rule.
- **"If it's not right, don't do it"** implies a **Positive Ethic** (an obligation to do good/protect democracy). Positive ethics often violate the "energy rule" because they are hard to define and exhausting to fulfill.

- _Critique:_ Is the Marcus Aurelius motto a realistic code for a breaking news reporter or a filmmaker? Why or why not?
I think its a realistic quote based on a properly tested ethical code. Its incredibly vague and requires one to have sharp lines drawn in their ethical code. I think to our previous classes discussion on the hospital that was possibly abusing their patients. The action of lying is not right, but if your ethical code states that you will break this code if that lie will help to save tens of hundreds of people then it is the correct action.
