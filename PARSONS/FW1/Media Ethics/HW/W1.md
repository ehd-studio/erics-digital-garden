### Part 4: Positive Ethics & The "Energy Rule"

Reference: "Draw Sharper Lines"

Negative ethics (Do not steal) require no energy. Positive ethics (Help the poor) require infinite energy unless you draw a line.

Draft ONE positive ethic relevant to your career, then "draw the line" to make it livable.

For example:  
• Draft: "I will help junior employees/students with their careers."

- The Line (Specific Limit): "I will dedicate ___ hours per month to mentoring," OR "I will help anyone who asks, provided they work within my immediate department."

Your Specific Limit:

## Know Who You Are: The Pendulum Damper Theory 

**Sit with these questions, jot down your thoughts and share your answers in class**

- *When faced with ethical dilemmas, moral conundrums, and internal conflicts, what dictates your actions? Is it fear, other people’s opinions, self-preservation, or your own values and principles?*

When thinking of recent examples, my actions skewed towards self-preservation in the face of others opinions. I took a position of not wanting to rock the boat and stand up for what is right largely because I didn't know in the moment what would be ethically sound. I also heavily considered how speaking out would affect the connection to others around me and wether instigating some form of conflict would be worth losing some favor. I instead took the position of playing both sides and regret sitting on the fence rather than discussing how their actions were wrong in my eyes.

- *Think of a time when your values conflicted with what was easiest, safest, or most popular. How did you decide what to do? How did it turn out? Would you make the same choice today?*

In the past, I've found myself trying to be a collectivist and choosing the groups opinions over my own ethics. This is a result from an ethics code that skews more towards positive ethics that are out of reach rather than a refined code that helps to guide my own personal reactions. I also realize that my decisions come from a code that is strongly connected to the judgement of others. This led to my actions feeling unfounded, shallow and unjustifiable upon reflection. Though I fear I would make the same mistake today, continuing to learn about ethical codes has helped me understand not only why I felt bad, but how I can prevent future actions that I will regret.
