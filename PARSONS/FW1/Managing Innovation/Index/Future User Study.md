The **Future User** study is a core method within the **Foresight Framework**, a proprietary methodology developed at Stanford University and tested with teams and organizations since 2004. The framework is designed to help organizations systematically scout future innovation opportunities and build forward-looking strategic plans.

---

### What is the "Future User" Study?

The Future User study is situated in **Phase II (Opportunity)** of the five-phase Foresight Framework.

- **The Goal:** To describe future user needs **without extrapolating biases from today’s users**. Often, product teams build for the future by simply projecting current habits, complaints, and baseline expectations forward. The Future User study strips away these contemporary biases to evaluate how shifting societal, demographic, or technical forces will create entirely new paradigms of user friction and desire.
- **The Outcome:** A structured **comparison of two customer/user profiles**, comparing current user expectations against anticipated future needs.

---

### How to Apply the Foresight Framework

To successfully run a Future User study, practitioners must apply it within the broader, sequential steps of the **Foresight Framework**, which guides teams through five distinct phases of problem scoping and solving:

#### Phase I: Perspective (Establishing the Past & Context)

Before jumping into the future, you must look backward to establish a baseline of change.

1. **Context Maps:** Create a visual map of the top 8 dimensions of your problem space to retain complexity while choosing priority areas.
2. **Progression Curves:** Connect related historical events to create timelines showing past developments.
3. **Janus Cones:** Build a large-scale pattern map of past major decisions and indirect influences within an era that shape current constraints.

#### Phase II: Opportunity (Designing the Future User)

With historical context established, you begin mapping the future customer.

1. **Generational Arcs:** Identify the target population cohorts and map their shared demographic values and attributes.
2. **Future User Profile Mapping:** Draft and contrast two distinct user profiles—one of today's customer and one of the future user. Document how their motivations, environment, and needs will diverge from current norms.
3. **Futuretelling:** Translate the future user profiles into a short, narrative performance (such as a live skit or video). This forces the team to capture nonverbal, environmental, and contextual details of how the future user will actually experience the product or service.

#### Phase III: Solution (Narrowing the Path)

Define the concrete questions and roadmaps needed to make the opportunity real.

1. **White Spots:** Evaluate the competitive landscape and filter emerging market areas to pinpoint the exact future opportunity space.
2. **Paper Mockups:** Build 3D physical or system prototypes that demonstrate interactions, workflows, and how different components relate.
3. **Change Paths:** Prioritize immediate decisions and chart a high-level, visual roadmap to reach the desired future first.

#### Phase IV: Team (Aligning Leadership and Talent)

Identify the talent and networks required to execute the vision.

1. **Buddy Checks:** Rehearse the communication of your ideas with partners to refine responses to criticism.
2. **VOICE Stars:** Assess your team’s internal culture and capacity for radical innovation across five critical dimensions.
3. **Crowd Clovers:** Map your personal and organizational stakeholder networks to identify key players needed to bring the innovation to life.

#### Phase V: Vision (Sharpening the Long-Term Strategy)

Equip the organization with a shared North Star.

1. **Vision Statement:** Formulate a short, inspirational narrative of the product's ultimate future value.
2. **DARPA Hard Test:** Run a breakthrough-potential test to evaluate if your concept has true, high-impact visionary potential.
3. **Pathfinders:** Visual planning to navigate internal corporate alignment and clear a friction-free path to launch.

### Integrating with Modern Anticipatory Design

When applying the "Future User" concept to modern technology (especially artificial intelligence and adaptive software), product teams often pair the Stanford methodology with the **Anticipatory Design Framework**.

While the Stanford Foresight Framework helps identify _who_ the future user is, Anticipatory Design dictates _how_ software should behave for them. It breaks down into three parallel phases: **Anticipate, Imagine, and Shape**. Instead of trapping users in reactive setups—like blank prompt boxes and static forms that place 100% of the cognitive burden on the individual—it uses future-user foresight to design **Predictive UX**. The software infers context, anticipates behavioral readiness, and triggers timely, non-intrusive micro-interventions before the user even has to ask.
