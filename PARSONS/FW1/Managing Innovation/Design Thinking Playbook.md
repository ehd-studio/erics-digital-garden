## Chapter 1

### 1.1 What needs are addressed in the Design Thinking Playbook
- *[[User Profile Canvas]]* - understanding a users background through investigative questioning
	- Used for strategy work and mapping the associated business ecosystems
- These are methods for when you are able to identify your user base and want to dive deeper into empathizing/understanding your user base

#### Understanding your users
- 1. Find the User 
	- Quant data collection
- 2. Build up a hypothesis
	- Descriptions of similar groups
- 3. Confirmations
	- Quant data collection, Empathy Maps
- 4. Finding Patterns
	- Jobs-to-be-done method, Categorization
- 5. Creating Personas
- 6. Define Situations
	- User Profile Canvas, Customer Journey
- 7. Validation
	- Interviewing personas, reading about personas
- 8. Dissemination of Knowledge
	- Posters, meetings, emails, etc.
- 9. Creating Scenarios
	- Narrative scenarios, Hook method
- 10. Continuous further development
	- Usability testing, updating personas with new information

#### Canvas Methods
- *[[Empathy Mapping]]* - discussing a users experience and empathizing with each emotion they discuss
	- Good to involve images and video if allowed
	- Discuss how they are feeling
- *[[AEIOU Framework]]* - add structure to the format of user questioning and observation
	- Activities: What are people doing? What is their task? What activities do they carry out?
	- Environment: What does the environment look like?
	- Interaction: How do they interact amongst each other? What interfaces do they interact with?
	- Objects: Who uses the objects and in what environment?
	- User: Who are the users? Who influences them? What role do the users play?
- *[[Hook Framework]]* - how a framework of action is triggered and continually entered through reward
	- Investigating how to optimize each section:
	- *Trigger* - (internal) Loneliness triggering a move to Facebook (external) a notification triggering a user to check Facebook
		- Internal - What does the user want and how do we make it more effective?
		- External - What relevant triggers of action can be deployed?
	- *Action* - What is the simplest action a user needs to perform in order to be rewarded?
	- *Variable Reward* - Is there a reward for the user and/or the community?
	- *Investment* - How does our user release their action and re-enter the loop? Is there a way to close the loop more effectively?
- *[[Jobs-to-be-done]]*
	- 1. *When...* - Action taken ("When I take a photo")
	- 2. *I want to...* - Desire ("I want to edit it like a professional")
	- 3. *so I can...* - Outcome ("So I can have beautiful photos")
- *[[Future User Study]]*
	- When a user is not at their stage of life we are analyzing so you will need to make some judgement calls

### 1.2 Why is Process Awareness Key?
- At what point of time do we need to collect information and ideas? 
- At what point do we need to synthesize those ideas into distinct solutions?
- **Micro cycle** - day to day operation of the project and the cycle put in place to build
- **Macro cycle** - the overall project roadmap
#### The Design Thinking MICRO-cycle
- Diverging is often the easiest part to spend time on
- Converging is difficult with more information and ideas
- *The Groan Zone* is the apex of when we flip from Diverging to Converging
	- Creating a tighter time window can allow for more distinct and quick decision making where ideas are connected, deleted and decided upon quicker
- It is good to bring in other parties so the idea is not singular to your group
- There are a lot of different Design Thinking Processes that achieve similar outcomes through different methods (find the best one that suits the project)
	- IDEO Design Thinking Process (Understand, Observe, Visualize, Evaluate, Implement)
	- Scrum or ISO 13407
	- ICT Swisscom (Hear, Create, Deliver)
#### Phases of the Microcycle
- **Understand** - Understanding a problem or persona on the way to a problem statement
	- WHY - exploratory
	- HOW - specific
- **Observe** - Investigating the group we need to deal with first hand
- **Define a point of view** - Unify and establishing a common knowledge base through previous gained information
- **Ideate** - Brainstorming and creation for functions irrespective of constraints
- **Prototype** - Moving ideas to actionable prototypes
- **Test** - Large scale testing with users
- **EXTRA: Reflect** - Wraps up the process after completion with a retrospection

#### The Design Thinking MACRO-cycle
- **Diverge**
	- Emphasize creative process in order to get any and all ideas to run rather than start constrained
	- *1. Initial ideas are worked out in brainstorming*
		- Understanding others opinions
		- Sprints to work through thought process
	- *2. Develop critical functionalities for the user*
		- Identify what is essential for the target user base
	- *3. Finding benchmarks from other industries and experiences*
		- Collecting and connecting experiences or similar situations in other industries
	- *4. Heighten creativity and find a dark horse solution*
		- Push team members to find solutions when main functionalities are taken out
	- *5. Create a funky prototype*
		- Maximize learning success and minimize time/attention by building a prototype without any constraints (what does/doesn't work)
	- *6. Determine the idea with the vision prototype*
		- Synthesize based on all previous ideas and steps of understanding, observation, POV, ideation and prototyping
		- Test and learn from you users what did and did not work
- **Converge**
	- Based on the response and ideas, move towards a more functional prototype
	- *1. Concretize the vision step by step*
		- Functional prototype - create an MVP (minimal vital product) on the outset to concretize the essentials and test them extensively with users


### 1.3 How to get a good problem statement
- **Well-defined Problem** - Well defined problem, multiple paths, one solution
- **Ill-defined Problem** - Multiple problems, multiple paths, multiple solutions
- **Wicked Problem** - Unknown problem, multiple paths, part of a solution that helps make the problem statement more clear
- Finding the design challenge can be defined using a couple different frameworks:
	- *How Might We...*
	- *Change the Perspective* - What if...? What might be possible?
	- *Experiencing other products to discover design challenge*
