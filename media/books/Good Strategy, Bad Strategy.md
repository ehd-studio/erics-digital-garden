## Chapter 1: Good Strategy is unexpected
### Apple and Operation Desert Storm
- Goals and initiatives are not a strategy
- Many have goals, but strategy to accomplish those goals cohesively is the first thing that can set you apart
- Surprise at the existence of a strategy is a HUGE advantage
- Streamlining strategy to one lane creates flexibility to take advantage of new opportunity
	- Being able to not be the first to do it, but the first to get it right

### Apple
- 1980s Apple was in a nose dive
- Product line was too complicated and over bloated selling to too many people
- Steve Jobs was brought back and thinned out company
	- redefined the offerings to a streamlined set of products/services that could exploit and grow quickly
	- Brought faster processors from NeXT
	- Instituted production model based in Asia like Dell
	- Created compatibility with Windows OS
	- Cut 5/6 retailers who were selling at multiple price points
	- Started to sell through online platform
	- Cut all models except for one personal computer
- Having the quickness, flexibility and cleverness to jump at 'the next big thing'
	- iPod, Apple Music, iPhone

### Desert Strom
- Successful deception (placement of troops, media coverage)
- Most complex organizations spread rather than focus resources
- Focused strategy for large organizations (US Military, Apple) is unexpected
	- Pureness is strategy
- Schwarzkopf had to have dissuade desires of other organizations/groups (Allies, Airforce, US pundits, Saudi coalition) in order to have a pure push for strategy for central command
	- Wide variety of actions and goals is a luxury of the rich and made

## Chapter 2: Discovering Power
### David and Goliath
- The story is a thought exercise on business analysis. The outcome of the story is a testament to not taking business structure at face value
	- Relative strength to weakness in business is a shallow approach
	- Story is about the *discovery of power* - how someone can see what others are not
- Preconceived ideas of strength and weakness is unsound
- *Pre Fight Analysis*
	- David
		- Pros - Brave
		- Cons - Small, Weak, In-experience
	- Goliath
		- Pros - Strong, Experienced, Brave
		- Cons - Unknown
- *Post fight after David has slain Goliath*
	- David
		- experienced with Shepards sling, youthful quickness, discarded armor for quickness and general lack of effectiveness if Goliath hit him
	- Goliath
		- weakness near head with lack of armor
	- David's ranged weapon takes away strength advantage of Goliath and exploits his biggest disadvantage (lack of head protection)

### Walmart and KMart
- Using Goliath and David story as a case study for Walmart growth over time
- Walmart system at face value is not something KMart couldn't replicate (data, digital inventory system, shipping, etc)
	- Why couldn't it be replicated?
- Walmart's advantage is either
	- there is a legal claim (patent)
	- cannot replicate
	- KEY: do not recognize and will not replicate due to inertia/incompetence (David's key advantages against Goliath)
