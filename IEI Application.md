## Details

### Diversity, Equity, Inclusion, Social Justice, Social Impact
The New School (TNS) is committed to promoting diverse perspectives and creating social and environmental impact through entrepreneurship. These are central to the university’s core values. 
The mission of the Impact Entrepreneurship Initiative (IEI) is to promote a shift in the practice of organizational formation and collaboration from extractive to generative values. As a university-wide initiative, we believe in advancing democratic principles and mutual and equitable relations. Together we develop just entrepreneurial skills and learn to employ inclusive design approaches to bring about innovation in the world. By challenging the status quo, we center our efforts on strengthening relationships with one another and in our communities, aiming to see and design with respect, care, and concern.
The Impact Entrepreneurship Initiative Fellowship seeks students who have demonstrated a capacity and willingness to design with and not for community. We seek students who are engaged with communities that have historically been excluded from traditional start-up ecosystems. To this end, IEI seeks applicants who exhibit a desire to learn deeply about the systemic issues faced by the communities they intend to support, and create relationships and structures to improve how we interact with intractable and complex systems.
Known as one of the most international universities in the United States, The New School has students from all corners of the world. Our New York City location provides an opportunity to build connections and relationships with community partners, thereby strengthening their local and global networks. We aim to nurture students who are invested in facilitating the co-creation of new, inclusive economic models that advance social and racial justice, with a shared commitment to respecting the dignity and agency of all people we encounter on our learning journey. IEI welcomes students who embrace a generous and generative vision for shared prosperity and a fervent desire to counter enduring oppressive systemic structures.
### Impact Entrepreneurship Fellowship (IEI) Values
As articulated in the Universal Declaration of Human Rights (Article 27, Section 1): “Everyone has the right to freely participate in the cultural life of the community, to enjoy the arts and to share in scientific advancement and its benefits.”
IEI engages, understands, and draws on a variety of persons and perspectives. The issues/problems we aim to address can be found by looking beyond our similarities, finding value in our differences, treating people with dignity and respect, and working on common causes toward a greater public good. 
We promote practices to ensure fair treatment, equality of opportunity, and parity in access to information and resources. Equal is defined as being the same or exactly alike, or characterized by equality. Fair is defined as just or appropriate. Equity, however, recognizes that individuals embody distinct experiences and circumstances, and to achieve parity, in practice, we acknowledge that an individual may need to experience or receive something different in order to achieve fairness while moving towards parity, fairness, and impartiality.
Our Fellowship challenges existing structure(s) and makes the transformational change necessary within our environs to ensure fair access and distribution of resources; inclusion requires, in both intent and practice, a commitment to fairness in power distribution and decision making.
 IEI seeks to enhance systems to support the agency and autonomy of people with disabilities, enabling individuals with disabilities to participate fully by providing flexible and adaptive points of access. 
We embody practices that challenge institutions and systems to ensure more just and fair outcomes. The Initiative fosters the development of innovative entrepreneurial approaches to address complex social, economic, and environmental challenges. From ideation to implementation and ongoing care, we create value for beneficiaries, communities, and customers. We create system within the limits of our biosphere, while maintaining or regenerating natural, social, and economic capital.

### Fellowship Commitment
**First-Year Requirements**
Fellows must actively participate in the year-long Impact Entrepreneurship Fellowship during their first academic year. This includes enrolling in the Impact Entrepreneurship Fellowship Cohort Course in the Fall semester and Venture Lab in the Spring semester, earning a grade of B or higher in each course to remain in good standing.

**Second-Year Eligibility**
To continue into the fellowship’s second year and receive the $5,000 award, fellows must earn a grade of B or higher in Venture Lab and submit a detailed service blueprint and timeline for their summer and second-year independent projects. The fellowship co-directors must approve this plan before the end of the Venture Lab course.

**Second-Year Structure**
The second year is self-directed, with mandatory check-ins and presentations or pitches to the community and mentors. Fellows must demonstrate measurable progress toward developing a Minimum Viable Product (MVP) that is ready for testing or actively being tested. Consistent, timely communication with the fellowship co-directors is required throughout the full two-year program.

**Enrollment & Funding**
Fellows must make satisfactory progress toward their master’s degree and remain enrolled in at least 6 credits per semester. Part-time students are eligible to participate; however, fellowship funding of $5,000 per year is only available for the first two years, for a maximum total of $10,000.
